# Audience-building strategy

The trap with "newsletter + social from zero" is that you're opening a shop on an empty street. The fix isn't building an audience — it's *borrowing* audiences that already exist.

## Three angles, in order of leverage

### 1. The 70 theatres are your distribution channel

Each venue has an email list, Instagram followers, and an audience who explicitly loves this stuff. If you become the aggregator they're happy to point at — because you send them traffic, mention their shows weekly, give them an embeddable widget — you turn them into a 70-source promotional engine.

**First action:** cold-email 5–10 venues with a clear value exchange. They want bums in seats; you want eyes on listings. The trade is obvious.

### 2. SEO

"Cheap theatre London this weekend," "fringe theatre near [neighbourhood]," "alternatives to West End" — high-intent searches with no dominant answer. Each show is a potential SEO landing page. Compounds quietly while you do other things.

**First actions:**
- Structured data (schema.org `Event`) on every show page
- Landing pages by neighbourhood and genre
- Sitemap submitted to Google Search Console

### 3. Be useful in public

r/london, r/uktheatre, theatre Twitter, Facebook groups for London culture. People ask *"what should I see this weekend?"* constantly. Genuine answering — not link-dumping — builds trust slowly but durably.

## Automation stack

Once channels prove out:

- **Email:** Beehiiv or Buttondown — both handle scheduled sends and automation cleanly
- **Social:** Buffer or Make.com to push new listings from the database to social
- **Approach:** scrape once, distribute everywhere

## What doesn't solve the cold-start problem

Neither broadening scope nor switching channels solves the underlying problem. A GPT in the store still needs discovery — same problem, different shop window. The three angles above are the actual work. Channel innovation (the AI/MCP layer) *amplifies* them but doesn't replace them.
