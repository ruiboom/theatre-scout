# Positioning and naming

## The wedge: "take a punt"

The site's value proposition has three angles, all true:

- **Cheaper** than West End (£10–£25 vs £80+)
- **More ambitious** programming
- **Take a punt** — variable quality reframed as discovery

The product can serve all three. The marketing can't lead with all three; *"we do everything"* reads as *"we do nothing in particular."*

The "punt" angle is the strongest wedge:

- Most distinctive (nobody else is saying it)
- Reframes variable quality as a feature (adventure, discovery), not a bug
- The cheapness and ambition both become *supporting evidence* rather than competing claims

**Tagline contender:** *Anywhere but the West End.*

**Brand sentence:** *"There are two theatre scenes in London. One has billboards. The other is where the interesting work happens."*

## On naming

`Anywhere But The West End` captures the wedge perfectly but has issues as a brand name:

- Long for a URL
- Defines by negation (what you're *not*)
- "Off West End" already exists (The Offies awards)

### Workshop shortlist

| Name | Strength | Risk |
|------|----------|------|
| **Punters** | Triple pun (audience / bet / Thames boat); short, very British, captures wedge | Gambling-adjacent might feel low-rent to funders |
| **B-Side** | Music-nerd reference (suits founder); West End = A-side, fringe = B-side | Under-40s may not know what a B-side is |
| **Tip-Off** | Insider-recommendation framing; easy URL | Slightly generic |
| **The Cheap Stalls** | Theatre vocab + value promise; sounds like a brand | Leads with "cheap" — undersells ambition half |
| **Deep Cuts** | Music ref again; "what real fans know" | Same crossover-confusion risk as B-Side |

### Status

**Landed on `Theatre Scout`** (May 2026) — the same name `scout/` already used. Pragmatic over poetic: the slug `theatre-scout` was already on github.com/ruiboom/theatre-scout, the User-Agent had been hitting venues for months as `TheatreScout/0.1`, and the design system's `.ts-` class prefix meant a rebrand-to-anything-else would have fought the existing assets. *"Anywhere but the West End"* survives as the strapline / About-page hook — the wedge messaging is what mattered, not the literal name.

The shortlist below is preserved for reference / future workshop fodder.

## On scope

Considered broadening from theatre to "independent culture" (music + sports).

**Decision: don't.** Sharp niche → big audience beats broad pitch → small audience nearly every time. *Anywhere But The West End* is a knife. *"Independent London culture"* is a butter spreader. Music and sports already have aggregators (Songkick, Dice, Skiddle); fringe theatre is the genuinely underserved gap.

**Compromise position:** keep theatre as the hero category, but frame the brand voice as "London's independent scene" with occasional adjacent flavour (a gig, a comedy night, an indie wrestling event). Positioning move, not scope move.
